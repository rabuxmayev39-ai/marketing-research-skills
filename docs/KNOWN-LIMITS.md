# Known limits

Read this before you rely on a skill in front of a client.

## How the skills were made
Each method skill was written by an AI model after reading free, credible pages for that method (authors' pages, open-access articles, institutes, professional bodies) and checked against a written brief. Each skill's **Sources** section lists what was opened. Where a source could not be opened, the skill says so.

## What was not done
- **Paid books and articles were not opened.** The content comes from free summaries and the authors' own pages. Where the sheet or the skill says "paid", treat the procedure as a faithful outline, not a substitute for the book.
- **Pages were read through a summarising tool**, so exact figures can be wrong. Check numbers (survey percentages, chart values, platform figures, sample sizes) at the source before quoting them. Highest-risk areas: S6-18, S8-04, S8-05, S9-04, S9-05.
- **The system has not been run on a live client project.** How well the orchestrator picks and mixes methods in practice is unproven.
- **Spreadsheet formulas in S3-09 were not run.**

## Working rules
Anything labelled **Working rule** is a threshold or formula chosen for practical use, not a published standard. Examples: cut-off lines in S6-04, the bottleneck ratio in S6-03, the 30 to 50 percent share in S6-13, the scoring in S0-08, S0-09, S0-10 and S0-13, the reach formula in S9-03, the budget formula in S9-05, the proportional difference-in-differences in S7-02.

## Thin or second-hand sources
S0-02 and S0-12 (summaries by third parties), S6-13 and S6-16 (blogs and columns), S6-07, S6-09, S6-10 and S0-05 (partly Wikipedia), S6-17 (no independent critique found), S7-01 (a trade-body report read second-hand), S8-01 and S8-03 (abstracts only), S1-03, S1-12, S1-22, S2-05, S2-10, S2-11, S2-12 and S4-18, S4-19 (limited sources; see each skill).

## Uzbekistan specifics
- S8-01, S8-02, S8-03 and S0-09 state no facts about Uzbekistan by design; they give questions to check locally.
- S8-04 to S8-07 point to live data sources that change. Always open the live page; the skills' example figures are labelled as examples. Some pages (for example parts of the statistics agency site) did not open when checked on 12 November 2026.
- Uzbek and Russian example quotes and questions were written by an AI. Have a native speaker read them before use.

## Evidence grades
A = tested in peer-reviewed research. B = established professional standard. C = practitioner tool with little formal testing. Local = the agency's own cases. The orchestrator never lets a grade C method stand alone as the basis for a decision.
