# DataReportal Digital Uzbekistan (S8-04)

DataReportal is a free online library of country reports on internet, mobile and social media use, written by Simon Kemp of the consultancy Kepios and published with the sponsors We Are Social and Meltwater. It has one page per country; the Uzbekistan page lists a report for every edition from Digital 2017 to Digital 2027. The newest, Digital 2027: Uzbekistan, carries the date 5 November 2026 and uses data described as late 2026 (checked 12 November 2026). DataReportal does not run its own survey in Uzbekistan. It gathers figures from other organisations and adds its own analysis. On the pages opened, the named providers were the United Nations (population), GSMA Intelligence (mobile connections), the ITU (internet users), Ookla (connection speeds) and the advertising tools of the platforms themselves: Meta for Facebook, Instagram and Threads, plus TikTok, LinkedIn, Reddit and X. Its Notes on Data page says sources include research firms, platforms, governments and DataReportal's own extrapolations.

Evidence grade: B. Grades are A = tested in peer-reviewed research, B = established professional standard, C = practitioner tool with little formal testing. DataReportal is a widely used industry reference that is open about its sources and their weaknesses, which is why it is B and not C. It is not A: it is a compilation, the platform numbers are what advertising tools report and not audited counts, and nobody outside the platforms can verify them. The main limit is that platform figures are advertising reach (the number of accounts an advertiser could show an ad to), not unique people. Use the figures for orders of magnitude and for comparing platforms inside one report; do not present them as a census of users.

Working rules for the assistant: answer in the user's language (Uzbek, Russian or English). Label FACT (a figure read on the live page today, with its date) versus INTERPRETATION (any estimate built on it). Open the live source every time; never reuse the example figures in this skill. Do not state any figure about Uzbekistan from memory. When done, return to the research-orchestrator skill.

## When to use it / When not to use it
Use it when:
- Desk research needs a first, rough picture of how many people are online and which platforms sell advertising in Uzbekistan.
- A media plan needs the size of a platform's advertising audience and its split by sex or age band, as the platform reports it.
- The team wants to compare platforms with each other inside the same report.

Do not use it when:
- The question is how many unique people use a platform, or how often. The source does not measure that.
- The platform that matters is missing from the report. Working rule: in the summary of the Digital 2027 Uzbekistan page read for this skill, no Telegram figure appeared; check the live page, and if a platform is absent, the report says nothing about it.
- A trend over several years is needed. The source itself warns against comparing editions.
- Regional figures inside Uzbekistan are needed. The report pages opened gave national figures only.

## What you need before starting (inputs)
1. The decision the number will feed (budget, channel choice, market size) and the target group (age, sex, place).
2. Internet access to open datareportal.com today.
3. The client's own data if any (ad account reach estimates, site analytics) for cross-checking.
4. A place to log the figure: the facts, assumptions, unknowns register (S0-13).

## Procedure
1. Open https://datareportal.com/digital-in-uzbekistan and pick the newest report. Write down its title, its publication date and the date the data refer to. If the page cannot be opened, say so and stop; do not fill the gap from memory.
2. Open https://datareportal.com/notes-on-data and read the entries for the edition in use. It lists, by date, changes in sources and methods (for example revised UN population figures, platforms changing how they report advertising reach). Note any entry that touches the figure you need.
3. For each figure, identify what it counts. Internet users: an estimate of individuals, built from sources that the Notes say can lag by a year or more; for Digital 2027, where no 2026 data existed, the change was set to zero. Mobile connections: SIM connections, not people; one person can hold several, and the report says some carry voice and SMS only. Social media user identities: accounts, renamed from users in April 2023 because the figure may not equal unique people.
4. Apply the reading traps for platform figures:
   - Advertising reach is not active users. The Notes say reach can differ widely from a platform's own monthly active user counts, for example because some users never see ads or ads are not sold in a country.
   - Duplicate and non-human accounts. One person may hold several accounts, and some accounts belong to businesses, pets or causes. The report shows platform numbers as given, even when they exceed the population.
   - The social media total is not a sum of people across platforms. Check how it was built. Example of what the source contained on the date checked, re-check before use: Digital 2027: Uzbekistan (5 November 2026) gave 16.2 million social media user identities and 16.2 million for Instagram advertising reach, so the total matched the largest single platform.
   - Age limits differ by platform. On the page read, Meta platforms, Reddit and X cover ages 13 and over, while the TikTok and LinkedIn tools report ages 18 and over only. LinkedIn counts registered members, not active ones. Do not rank platforms without stating the age base.
   - Gender is reported as female and male only; for X it is inferred, which the report calls less reliable.
5. Do not build your own trend from two editions. The source says that changes in sources, base revisions and corrections make figures not directly comparable, and that a fall may be a correction and not a real decline. If a change over time is needed, use the change printed inside the newest report and say it is the source's own figure.
6. Convert the figure into a planning number in separate, visible steps, each labelled Working rule: narrow to the target place and age band using the platform's own advertising tool; apply a stated discount for duplicate and non-human accounts; apply the share likely to be buyers in the period (S6-06). Give a range, not a single number. Working rule: no discount rate comes from the source, so every discount is an assumption to be tested.
7. Cross-check against a second source (S2-07): the client's ad account reach estimate, national statistics (S8-07), or a survey. Report disagreement instead of averaging it away.
8. Cite in the format below and log it in S0-13. Citation format: DataReportal / Kepios, with We Are Social and Meltwater. [Report title]. Published [date]; data refer to [date]. [URL]. Checked [date]. DataReportal's own citation page asks for sponsors, year, report title, the report URL and the retrieval date; the format here contains all of them.
9. Reuse limits: the citation page allows republishing single report pages whole and unaltered, with credit and a link, without charging for access; it asks for contact before using ten or more charts, and it does not supply raw data. Then return to research-orchestrator.

## Worked example
INVENTED EXAMPLE - every number below is invented and is not a statistic about Uzbekistan. Input: a furniture maker wants a cautious count of sofa buyers it could reach on one platform in one city this year.

```
Step                                              Number      Label
Platform advertising reach, whole country      1,000,000      invented "source figure"
In the target city (platform ad tool: 40%)       400,000      Working rule, from ad tool
Minus duplicate and non-human accounts (20%)     320,000      Working rule, assumption
Aged 25 to 45 (ad tool: 50%)                     160,000      Working rule, from ad tool
Buying a sofa this year (5%, to be tested)         8,000      assumption (S6-06)
Reached at least once by the plan (60%)            4,800      planning number
Cautious range (discount 10% to 30%)       5,400 to 4,200     report this range
```
Arithmetic check: 1,000,000 x 0.40 = 400,000; 400,000 x 0.80 = 320,000; 320,000 x 0.50 = 160,000; 160,000 x 0.05 = 8,000; 8,000 x 0.60 = 4,800. Range: 400,000 x 0.90 x 0.50 x 0.05 x 0.60 = 5,400; 400,000 x 0.70 x 0.50 x 0.05 x 0.60 = 4,200.
Output wording: FACT is only the source figure and its date. Everything below it is INTERPRETATION built on four assumptions, each listed with the test that would check it.

## Common mistakes
- Writing "X million people use the platform" when the source gives advertising reach.
- Adding platform figures together to get "total social media users".
- Comparing this year's report with an older one and calling the difference growth or decline.
- Ranking platforms with different age bases (13 and over against 18 and over) as if they were equal.
- Treating mobile connections as the number of phone owners.
- Concluding a platform is small because it is missing from the report.
- Copying a figure from this skill, an old deck or memory instead of the live page.

## Quality checklist
- Was the live report opened today, and are its publication date and data date recorded?
- Is each figure labelled as advertising reach, identities, connections or estimated individuals?
- Was the Notes on Data page checked for changes that affect the figure?
- Is the age base stated for every platform figure?
- Is every step from source figure to planning number shown and labelled Working rule or assumption?
- Is the result a range, with a second source checked against it?
- Is the citation complete, including the date checked?

## Notes for Uzbekistan
- The report is in English. When reporting in Uzbek or Russian, keep the source's English term next to the translation the first time (for example "advertising reach"), so the meaning is not lost (S3-03).
- Platforms that sell little or no advertising through the tools DataReportal reads may be under-counted or absent. Working rule: for messaging apps and local services, look for another source and say the report is silent.
- The report pages opened gave national figures. Tashkent versus regions has to come from the platform's advertising tool or from national statistics, and should be treated as an estimate.
- Labour migrants and temporary residents: the Notes say platforms may count people whom official population figures do not, which can push reach above the eligible population.
- Do not state facts about Uzbek consumers from this source beyond what the dated report says.
