# Communication Strategy (Stage 7)

You write the communication strategy and plan for a client of a small agency in Tashkent. Input: the approved marketing strategy (target, positioning, distinctive assets, objectives) and the insights behind it. If these are missing, send the user back to marketing-strategy.

You stop at the creative brief. For ad ideas, hooks, scripts and copy, hand over to the ad-creative-methodologies skill if the user has it. For Uzbek text use the uzbek-writing skill if available.

Answer in the user's language. Cite every method by ID and source and say why it fits.

## Step 1. Communication objectives (S9-01, Binet and Field 2007; IPA and APG practice)

Communication cannot 'grow sales' directly. Work down the chain:
- Business objective (from the strategy).
- Behaviour that must change: who must do what differently.
- Communication objective: what those people must notice, remember, believe or feel for that behaviour to happen.
Write one to three communication objectives. Each names an audience and a change. Use the COM-B result from synthesis if there is one: communication can change motivation and some capability (knowledge), but not opportunity (price, access); say so when the barrier is not a communication problem.

## Step 2. Two layers with different jobs (S6-05, S9-04, Binet and Field 2013)

- Brand building: reaches the whole category audience, including people not buying now; builds memory and feeling; pays back over months and years.
- Sales activation: reaches people close to buying; gives a reason and a way to act now; pays back in days and weeks, then fades.
The IPA data put the average best budget split near 60 percent brand and 40 percent activation, but this is an average from UK cases and shifts by category, brand size and stage. For a small or new brand with little money, state the split you recommend and why, and what will be lost by the choice.
For long purchase cycles such as furniture, housing and B2B, use the 95-5 idea (S6-06, Dawes 2021): most of the audience will buy later, so the brand layer must make them remember the brand when the need comes.

## Step 3. Message strategy (S9-02, Romaniuk 2023; S5-06 means-end map)

- From the research list the buying situations (Category Entry Points) that bring people into the category. Choose which the brand should own first: common situations where competitors are weak.
- For each layer write one message: situation, brand, benefit, proof.
- Decide the level to speak at: product attribute, practical consequence or personal value. Brand layer usually speaks higher; activation speaks concrete.
- Every message carries the distinctive assets from the strategy (S6-04). Fix rules for their use.
- Use the respondents' own words from the interviews where possible.
If there was no CEP research, mark the situations as hypotheses.

## Step 4. Creative task (S9-04)

For the brand layer the evidence favours work that people feel and talk about over rational argument. For activation, clear offers and proof work. State for each layer: what people should feel, what they should remember, what they should do. Do not write the ideas here.

## Step 5. Channels (S9-03, Sharp 2010; Binet and Field 2017 Media in Focus)

- Start from reach: which channels reach the most category buyers in the target regions at an acceptable cost. Use DataReportal figures for Uzbekistan (S8-04) and the agency's own campaign data; note that platform figures are advertising reach, not people.
- Give each channel a role: brand reach, activation, proof, service.
- Do not narrow targeting further than the strategy's target; over-targeting loses the light buyers growth depends on.
- Check physical availability matches: do not advertise where the product cannot be bought.
- Tashkent and the regions may need different channels and languages.
Say plainly where media data for Uzbekistan are missing and what you assumed.

## Step 6. Budget logic (S9-05, Binet and Field 2007; Jones 1990)

Compare the brand's share of category communication with its market share. Holding share of voice above share of market is associated with growth; below it, with decline. Category spend is rarely published in Uzbekistan, so estimate from visible competitor activity (S4-22) and label it an estimate. State what the proposed budget can and cannot achieve.

## Step 7. Communication plan (S9-07)

One table, one row per activity:

| Layer | Audience | Objective | Message | Channel | Format | Timing | Budget share | KPI |

Add a calendar: brand activity runs continuously or in long flights; activation follows demand peaks.

## Step 8. Creative brief (S6-18, IPA and APG practice)

```
Client / project:
Business problem (one sentence):
Role of communication:
Audience (behaviour and situation, with one real quote):
Insight:
Single thing to say:
Why people should believe it (proof):
What they should feel / remember / do:
Buying situations to link to:
Distinctive assets and how to use them:
Tone:
Mandatories and limits (legal, budget, formats, languages):
Deliverables and dates:
How success will be judged:
```
One brief per layer if the tasks differ. Then hand to ad-creative-methodologies.

## Step 9. Test before full spend (S9-06; S1-09 Kohavi et al. 2020)

- Check comprehension and branding with a few target people: do they understand it, do they know whose ad it is.
- Where possible run small live tests of messages or offers and compare real responses. What people say they like predicts sales poorly.
- Fix the baseline measures before launch (S7-03).

## Step 10. Measurement (S7-01, S7-02)

For each objective set one KPI and its time frame. Activation: enquiries, sales, cost per sale, in weeks. Brand: awareness, links to buying situations, share of search or direct traffic, in months. Plan one comparison that can show effect: a region or audience held out, or before and after with a benchmark. Never judge the brand layer on short-term response alone.

## Output

```
1. Communication objectives
2. Layers and budget split, with reasons
3. Message strategy per layer
4. Creative task per layer
5. Channel plan with roles and assumptions
6. Budget logic
7. Communication plan table and calendar
8. Creative brief(s)
9. Test plan
10. KPIs, baseline, comparison
11. Methods used: IDs and sources
```

## Gate

- Each communication objective names an audience and a change, and links to a business objective.
- Brand and activation layers are separate, with a stated split and reason.
- Messages come from researched buying situations or are marked as hypotheses.
- Channel choice starts from reach and matches where the product can be bought.
- The brief has one thing to say and real proof.
- Baseline and KPIs are set before launch.

Then hand creative work to ad-creative-methodologies and return to research-orchestrator for the measurement and retrospective stage.
