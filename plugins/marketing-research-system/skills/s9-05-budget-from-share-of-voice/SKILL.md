# Budget from share of voice (S9-05)

Share of voice (SOV) is a brand's share of all advertising in its category; share of market (SOM) is its share of category sales; excess share of voice (ESOV) is SOV minus SOM. This method uses the link between the three to size a budget: it works out what SOV a share target implies and what that SOV costs, given what competitors spend. John Philip Jones proposed comparing the two shares for budgeting in Harvard Business Review (1990). Les Binet and Peter Field (IPA, 2007 and later) reported that brands with SOV above SOM tend to grow and brands below tend to shrink, and a Nielsen study (2009) of 123 brands in 30 categories put an average on it: about 0.5 points of market share a year for every 10 points of ESOV. Measuring SOV and reading competitors is S4-22; this skill starts where that one ends and produces a budget range and an argument.

Evidence grade: A, for the direction of the relationship. Grades are A = tested in peer-reviewed research, B = established professional standard, C = practitioner tool with little formal testing. The pattern has been found in several large data sets; a journal study on it is listed in S4-22 and was not opened here. The size of the effect is much less certain. Nielsen stresses wide variation and advises brands to measure their own relationship: in its data market leaders gained about 1.4 points per 10 points of ESOV and challengers about 0.4. Binet and Field's B2B figure (0.7) rests on fewer than 50 award cases and they call it tentative. The data are mostly UK and Western consumer goods, partly from self-reported award entries. Published criticism: Ebiquity argues that SOV alone no longer predicts results, since attention, creative quality and media mix decide whether spend works, and that competitor spend is now hard to see in closed digital platforms; Byron Sharp rejects fixed share-of-voice quotas. Correlation is also not proof of cause: growing brands may simply spend more. In Uzbekistan category spend is rarely published, so SOV will be a proxy built from visible activity and must be labelled ESTIMATE.

Working rules for the assistant: answer in the user's language (Uzbek, Russian or English). Label FACT (the client's own spend and sales) versus INTERPRETATION (category spend estimates, the growth rate chosen). Always give a range, never one number. When the budget range is written, return to the research-orchestrator skill.

## When to use it / When not to use it
Use it when:
- A client asks "how much should we spend?" or sets a share target with a fixed budget.
- A budget cut is proposed and its likely cost in share must be shown.
- The budget has always been set as last year's figure or a percentage of sales.

Do not use it when:
- No estimate of category advertising exists. Run S4-22 first.
- Sales come from tenders, personal selling or distribution deals and the category barely advertises.
- The brand is new, with almost no share. The rule then gives only a rough floor; build the budget from the reach task (S9-03).

## What you need before starting (inputs)
1. SOM for the client and main rivals, and the client's SOV and ESOV with low and high estimates (S4-22).
2. Estimated total category communication spend for 12 months, with the method used (S4-22).
3. The client's current spend by layer, and the share target with its time frame (S9-01).
4. Whether the brand is the category leader or a challenger, and whether it is a launch.
5. What the client can afford: margin per unit and the owner's upper limit.

## Procedure
1. Confirm the frame from S4-22: same category, geography, period and channels for SOV and SOM. Split category spend into the client's spend and "others".
2. Compute the maintenance budget: the spend at which SOV equals SOM. The deck summarising Binet and Field's work treats SOV equal to SOM as the balance point. Formula (working rule, simple algebra): budget = target SOV x others' spend / (1 - target SOV).
3. Choose a growth rate and say why. Default: Nielsen's average of 0.5 share points a year per 10 points of ESOV. Run a second case with 0.4 if the client is a challenger. For B2B, Binet and Field's tentative figure is 0.7. Never apply a leader's rate to a challenger. Nielsen also found launches and relaunches gained 15 to 25 per cent more per point than the norm; working rule: do not rely on this bonus in the budget.
4. Convert the share target into required ESOV: required ESOV = yearly share gain wanted / rate x 10. Then target SOV = SOM + required ESOV.
5. Convert target SOV into a budget with the formula in step 2. Working rule: this assumes rivals keep spending as now; add a case where the main rival raises spend.
6. Carry the uncertainty through. Repeat steps 2 to 5 with the low and the high estimate of others' spend from S4-22. Report three figures per scenario: low, middle, high.
7. Compare with reality in three ways. (a) Affordability: is the budget within what margins allow? (b) Task: does it at least pay for the reach plan in S9-03? (c) Target: if the required budget is far beyond reach, the share target is not consistent with the money; say so and offer a smaller target or a longer time frame. This reverse use is often the most valuable result.
8. Add the quality caveat. Nielsen and Ebiquity both state that spend share works only with effective creative and a sensible media mix; Nielsen notes that challengers need stronger creative than leaders for the same result. Link the budget to S9-04 and S9-06.
9. For a proposed cut, run the rule backwards: new SOV, new ESOV, implied yearly share change at the chosen rate. Present it as an indication, not a forecast.
10. Write the budget argument in plain words: the balance point, the cost of the target, the range, and the assumptions. State that the rule sets total spend, not its split (S6-05) or its channels (S9-03). Plan to recheck SOV in six to twelve months (S4-22). Return to research-orchestrator.

## Worked example
INVENTED EXAMPLE - not real data. A juice brand in Uzbekistan, a challenger. From S4-22: SOM 8%; category communication spend about 20,000 million soums a year (ESTIMATE from counted activity, range plus or minus 25% on others' spend); own spend 1,200 million soums. The owner wants +1 share point a year.

```
NOW        SOV = 1,200 / 20,000 = 6%   ESOV = 6 - 8 = -2 points   others = 18,800
MAINTAIN   target SOV 8%     budget = 0.08 x 18,800 / 0.92   = 1,635 m soums
rabuxmayev39-ai'S TARGET +1 point a year, rate 0.5 per 10 points
           ESOV needed 20 -> SOV 28%   budget = 0.28 x 18,800 / 0.72 = 7,311 m
           -> about six times current spend: target and money do not fit
REVISED TARGET +0.25 point a year
  rate 0.5:  ESOV 5    -> SOV 13%     budget = 0.13 x 18,800 / 0.87   = 2,809 m
  rate 0.4:  ESOV 6.25 -> SOV 14.25%  budget = 0.1425 x 18,800 / 0.8575 = 3,124 m
RANGE for MAINTAIN if others spend 14,100 (low) or 23,500 (high):
           0.08 x 14,100 / 0.92 = 1,226 m      0.08 x 23,500 / 0.92 = 2,043 m
ARGUMENT                                                          INTERPRETATION
  Current spend is below the balance point even on the low estimate
  (1,200 < 1,226). Holding share needs roughly 1,200 to 2,000 m; growing by
  a quarter point a year needs roughly 2,800 to 3,100 m at the middle estimate.
```
Arithmetic check: 1,200 / 20,000 = 0.06; 20,000 - 1,200 = 18,800; 0.08 x 18,800 = 1,504 and 1,504 / 0.92 = 1,634.8; 1 / 0.5 x 10 = 20 and 8 + 20 = 28; 0.28 x 18,800 = 5,264 and 5,264 / 0.72 = 7,311.1; 7,311 / 1,200 = 6.1; 0.25 / 0.5 x 10 = 5 and 8 + 5 = 13; 0.13 x 18,800 = 2,444 and 2,444 / 0.87 = 2,809.2; 0.25 / 0.4 x 10 = 6.25 and 8 + 6.25 = 14.25; 0.1425 x 18,800 = 2,679 and 2,679 / 0.8575 = 3,124.2; 18,800 x 0.75 = 14,100 and 18,800 x 1.25 = 23,500; 0.08 x 14,100 = 1,128 and 1,128 / 0.92 = 1,226.1; 0.08 x 23,500 = 1,880 and 1,880 / 0.92 = 2,043.5. Cross-check: 2,809.2 / (18,800 + 2,809.2) = 0.13.

## Common mistakes
- Giving one budget figure with no range when the category estimate is a proxy.
- Applying the leader's growth rate, or the average, to a small challenger without a second case.
- Reading the rule as a guarantee: spend X, gain Y.
- Forgetting that the client's own extra spend enlarges the category total (use the formula, not SOV x old total).
- Assuming rivals will not respond.
- Using SOV to defend a budget while the creative is weak or unbranded.
- Mixing frames: national SOM with Tashkent-only SOV.
- Repeating the S4-22 analysis here instead of taking its output.

## Quality checklist
- Do SOV and SOM use one category, area and period?
- Is category spend labelled ESTIMATE with its method and a low-high range?
- Is the maintenance budget shown before any growth budget?
- Is the growth rate named, sourced and matched to leader or challenger status?
- Are low, middle and high budgets shown?
- Was the result compared with affordability and with the reach plan?
- Is the creative-quality caveat stated?
- Is a recheck date set?

## Notes for Uzbekistan
- Published category spend is rare. Use the visible-activity proxy from S4-22, say so on the first line, and never present the result as measured.
- Much activity may run through bloggers, Telegram channels and direct deals that are hard to count. Practical caution: widen the range when such channels dominate the category.
- Market share figures are often the owner's guess. Record them as estimates and test the conclusion with a lower and a higher SOM.
- Regional rivals may be invisible from Tashkent. Keep the frame to the area where both SOV and SOM can be estimated.
- All published rates come from Western markets. Treat 0.5 as a starting hypothesis and replace it with the client's own history as soon as two or three years of data exist.
